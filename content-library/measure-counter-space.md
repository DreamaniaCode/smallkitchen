# How to Measure Counter Space Before Buying an Appliance

Measure appliance footprint, opening movement and manual-specified operating clearance with a practical worksheet for small kitchen counters.

The most useful appliance measurement is not just width, depth or height. It is whether the equipment can sit, open and operate in the place you intend to use it while leaving the rest of the kitchen workable. Record those three stages separately before comparing products.

This is a planning worksheet, not a safety inspection. Use the exact appliance manual for placement, surfaces, clearances and electrical requirements. No generic allowance in this guide replaces those instructions.

## Define the usable counter zone

Measure the counter from side to side and from the wall or backsplash to the usable front edge. Note the height to the lowest upper cabinet, shelf or projecting fixture. Record obstacles such as a trim strip, outlet cover or fixed rack rather than treating the area as a perfect empty rectangle.

Then reserve the preparation area you need every day. A machine can fit physically while making the kitchen worse if it occupies your only place to chop ingredients or set a plate. Use the remaining area as the candidate zone. The amount to reserve is a personal workflow choice, not a universal number.

## Record the closed appliance dimensions

Use the exact model's manufacturer dimensions and identify what they include. Handles, tank protrusions, cords and accessories can complicate a product photograph. If the source does not explain a necessary dimension, ask the manufacturer before treating it as confirmed.

Keep the product name, US model number and source link beside the numbers. Do not silently transfer specifications between similarly named sizes or regional versions. [Hamilton Beach's model-specific coffee maker information](https://hamiltonbeach.com/coffee-makers) illustrates why refill design and model identification belong in the record as well as nominal capacity.

## Add opening movement as a separate line

Draw arrows showing what moves: lid upward, drawer forward, basket sideways or tank out and up. Measure the space available for that movement. Include room to grip and remove the component rather than only room for it to crack open.

Use a paper footprint and ordinary empty objects to rehearse the process before the appliance arrives. You are checking reach and layout, not simulating heat or proving a safe operating setup. A paper rectangle can reveal a crowded corner, but it cannot establish the manufacturer's ventilation requirement.

## Read the operating requirements

Write manual-specified requirements in their own column. Some measurements describe the body; others describe use with a lid, drawer or display extended. For example, [OXO's pull-out-display scale](https://www.oxo.com/more-oxo/5-lb-food-scale-with-pull-out-display.html) makes it easy to understand why closed size and working shape are different concepts, even for a relatively simple device.

For heating appliances, check the manual for surfaces, nearby objects and required clearance. Do not replace a missing instruction with a convenient number copied from another product. If the kitchen cannot meet a necessary requirement, remove that location from the plan.

## Complete the worksheet before ordering

Save these fields for every candidate: model; usable counter zone; closed width, depth and height; opening directions; documented extra operating room; cleanup access; and storage home. Add one sentence explaining what daily problem the purchase solves.

Compare two candidates using the same worksheet. Keep any unknown field visible instead of filling it with an estimate. A slightly larger body with better access can be more useful than a smaller object that blocks preparation. Choosing from verified dimensions and a realistic routine is the purpose of measuring; the smallest number is not the goal.
